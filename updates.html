Update 01 Project Foundation

Getting the Project Off the Ground

Before we had a car, most of the work happened behind a laptop. We put the team together, built this website and our sponsor packet, made a budget, and started reaching out to people and businesses who might want to help.

It was not the most exciting part of the project, but it gave us a real plan and eventually put us in a position to start seriously looking for a car.

Update 02 August 2026

We Bought the Mustang

After a lot of searching, we finally bought the car: a 2005 Ford Mustang GT Premium with Ford's 4.6L 3-valve V8 and a 5-speed manual transmission.

Buying it was the point where this whole project started to feel real. We can finally stop talking about what we might build and start taking apart an actual car.

We liked the Mustang because parts are widely available, there is a huge amount of information out there about the S197 chassis, and it gives us a relatively straightforward rear-wheel-drive V8 car to learn on. More importantly, we finally have something sitting in the garage that we can work on.

Our 2005 Mustang GT before the teardown began.

Update 03 September 2026 · Current

Tracking Down a Very Weird Engine Noise

Almost immediately, the Mustang gave us our first real problem: a very loud and irregular metallic clacking/slapping noise coming from the passenger-side front of the engine.

We pulled the passenger-side valve cover and started checking the valvetrain and timing area. We have looked at the cam, followers, springs, and the visible section of timing chain, and so far nothing up top looks catastrophically broken.

Right now, our best guess is that the noise is coming from the timing system, possibly a chain tensioner, guide, or another part of the timing drive. We have not confirmed that yet, so we are trying not to just throw parts at the engine.

Before we spend money on the repair, we want to make sure the rest of the engine is healthy too.

The encouraging part:

If we confirm that the timing system is the problem, it is a very fixable one. The front of the engine has to come apart, but the chains, guides, tensioners, and related parts can all be replaced. Those are also exactly the kinds of parts we would rather refresh now than trust for a 25-hour race.

Update 04 In Progress

Taking It Apart and Going Through Everything

While we figure out the engine noise, we have also started stripping the car and going through everything that will matter once it becomes a race car.

The factory seats, carpet, trim, sound deadening, and other interior pieces are coming out so we can inspect the chassis and eventually make room for the roll cage, racing seat, harness, fire system, and the rest of the safety equipment.

We are doing the same thing mechanically. Since we already have the car apart, we would much rather replace something questionable now than discover it halfway through the race.

Engine Finish the noise diagnosis and refresh the timing system and other worn components while we are already in there.
Oiling & Cooling Improve the systems that will have to keep the V8 alive for hours at a time under race conditions.
Safety Strip and prepare the interior for the cage, containment seat, harness, fire system, and other required equipment.
Everything Else Inspect the brakes, suspension, belts, hoses, seals, bearings, and other wear items before we trust the car on track.
Our goal is pretty simple: make the car finish.

We are not chasing a huge horsepower number. We would much rather have a slightly slower Mustang that can take 25 hours of abuse than a faster one sitting in the paddock with the hood up.
